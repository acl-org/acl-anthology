---
Title: How are Anthology proceedings submitted to scientific indexes?
category: content
weight: 4
---

Scientific indexes are curated databases of scholarly publications and citations.
Researchers and librarians use them to discover literature and trace citations across disciplines, while universities and funders use their data to assess research output and impact.
Some institutions, particularly in Asia and Europe, require or give extra weight to indexed publications for degrees, promotion, or funding.
Indexing does not guarantee an individual paper's quality, and a paper can be published and cited without it.

The ACL Anthology submits eligible proceedings to [Scopus](https://www.scopus.com/) and the [Web of Science](https://www.webofscience.com/).
This includes the main proceedings volumes of ACL conferences ([ACL](https://aclanthology.org/venues/acl/), [NAACL](https://aclanthology.org/venues/naacl/), [EACL](https://aclanthology.org/venues/eacl/), [AACL](https://aclanthology.org/venues/aacl/), and [EMNLP](https://aclanthology.org/venues/emnlp/)).
These submissions are handled by our [Indexing Lead volunteer]({{< relref "/info/credits.md#active-volunteers" >}}).
Unfortunately, we do not submit [Findings](https://aclanthology.org/venues/findings/) volumes, because the indices have refused them.
We are also unable to submit workshop proceedings or volumes from non-ACL venues.

Proceedings are normally submitted soon after they are published in the Anthology.
Submission does not mean that a volume will appear in an index immediately; the indices have their own processes, which are out of our control, and can take some time.

Chairs of other established venues may wish to pursue indexing themselves.
Begin by checking whether the venue is already covered and reviewing each index's current eligibility and selection criteria.
Scopus provides a [source suggestion form](https://suggestor.step.scopus.com/suggest-title), while Web of Science directs authorized publishers to request access to its [Publisher Portal](https://clarivate.com/academia-government/scientific-and-academic-research/publisher-solutions/new-users/).
Venue chairs should coordinate with the organization that publishes their proceedings, since an index may require the publisher, rather than an individual chair or editor, to make the request.
Be prepared to supply stable venue and publisher information, peer-review and editorial policies, publication history, and links to the proceedings and their metadata.
